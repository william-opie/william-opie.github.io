I've found online discourse about AI to be incredibly frustrating. There are endless posts from extremists on both sides. There seems to be no room for nuance.

[A post on my local subreddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/Spokane/comments/1v1ve7q/looking_for_a_new_furry_best_friend/) is a perfect example of this. Someone fostering a kitten for adoption used AI to generate a flyer. To me, this seems like a benign use of AI. The comments on the post are almost universally negative, lecturing the OP that they should've used Canva or some other tool to create the flyer without AI. Sprinkled in are vague complaints about AI being bad for the environment, as if one person's prompt to generate a basic flyer is going to tip us over the edge into climate change apocalypse.

The irony of folks posting on a social media site (that is hosted on servers in datacenters-- oh, the horror!) about AI being bad is rich.

To be clear, there are valid environmental concerns with datacenters. [Noise pollution](http://youtube.com/watch?v=RAliBRyq_1c), [water pollution](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jul/08/meta-datacenter-ai-wyoming-water), and concerns about [electricity costs rising](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/07/14/business/energy-environment/pjm-electricity-prices-data-centers.html) are real. However, these concerns aren't exclusive to AI datacenters, and associating all AI usage with these large datacenter projects ignores the growing number of open source models that can be entirely self-hosted (albeit [on prohibitively expensive hardware](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/glm-5.2#usage-guide) for most people).

Setting those concerns aside, I don't see creating flyers like this as the type of rewarding creative pursuit that most folks decry when complaining about AI usage. This isn't poster art for an event advertised by your local city government. It's not a logo for a small business or a mural on a building downtown. So why do we care if someone created it using AI?

I also find the obnoxiously pro-AI propaganda to be ridiculous. [Ed Zitron's blog](https://www.wheresyoured.at/) is filled with posts showing how [the economics of the AI hyperscalers don't add up](https://www.wheresyoured.at/the-openai-bubble/#the-ai-bubble-is-an-openai-bubble-%E2%80%94-to-a-mortal-end). Anthropic's CEO, Dario Amodei, has claimed that [software engineering is dead](https://x.com/aiedge_/status/2048130059412844995) many times. Software development has changed as AI has become more capable, but it's far from dead (as others have pointed out, we could likely see more demand for tech workers due to [Jevons paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jevons_paradox)).

AI is a tool. To broadly paint it as morally good or bad is inherently dumb. As a society, I wish we were more capable of grasping nuance. Nuance isn't sexy though, and social media algorithms don't boost moderate takes.
